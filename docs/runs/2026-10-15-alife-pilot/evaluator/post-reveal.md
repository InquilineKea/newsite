# Evaluator after reveal

The Explorer, Skeptic and Evaluator independently converge on a bounded favorable conclusion: these are useful discovery methods and a useful conceptual agenda. Finite observer-relative novelty, external evolutionary search, and orchestrated collaboration need separate evidence before being promoted to open-endedness, internal reproduction, or emergent cognition. This agreement is informative role convergence, not three independent model replications: the inherited model and source access are shared.

Complementary contributions are visible. Explorer separates the causal mechanisms and proposed endpoint. Skeptic exposes renderer/observer drift and the tension between stability and dynamic novelty. Evaluator identifies the replication unit, moving evaluation ruler, nested samples, and missing budget-matched comparison. The roles overlap on central cautions; that is useful consistency, but this three-paper run cannot establish a diversity advantage.

A remaining uncertainty is whether moving descriptors discover useful stepping stones that a fixed descriptor misses. Freezing the ruler may improve evaluation while weakening exploration. Keep adaptive discovery, but evaluate every checkpoint with an independently fixed ruler. Also report both invariant functional measures and visible variation: invariance deliberately discards some differences that a scientist might care about.

The proposed glider diagnostic illustrates measurement dependence. Translation-normalized glider states can collapse many absolute-position pixel patterns without generating any new mechanism. That would establish a property of this controlled example. It would not invalidate learned embeddings, replicate either paper, show that all translation distinctions are irrelevant, or establish a multiagent performance gain.

Strongest feasible next equal-budget experiment: benchmark the actual coordinator on held-out claim-audit packets. Randomize single-agent, independent-ensemble-plus-fixed-aggregation, homogeneous-team, and complementary-team arms. Give each identical paper text, source tools, total generated-token limit, and total tool calls. Include sealed first passes in every multiagent arm. Add a communication-disabled complementary arm to isolate interaction from extra sampling or roles. A fixed evaluator must be blind to arm and must not author the role briefs or see training packets.

Preregister the primary endpoint as correctly supported claim/limitation pairs per fixed budget; score exact source support, sample-unit errors, scope inflation and genuinely discriminating proposed tests. Deduplicate paraphrases. Use a small pilot to debug the rubric, then at least 20 previously withheld papers for an exploratory comparison; report per-paper effects and intervals, not sentence-level p-values. A time-limited panel of human adjudicators is stronger than our inherited-model judge. For this initial pilot, three papers are three clusters, not dozens of independent claims.

This is a proposed test. What is completed is the blind first-pass literature review and commitment/reveal workflow. No new ALife optimization, model-family comparison or autonomous-science performance experiment has been run.
