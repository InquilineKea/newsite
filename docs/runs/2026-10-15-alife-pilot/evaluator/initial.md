# Evaluator independent first pass

Scope: primary arXiv v1 texts 2412.17799, 2406.04235 and 2407.09502. This is a literature evaluation and a proposed falsification plan, not a reproduced experiment. No peer conclusions were read. Runtime inherits the parent model; role separation does not imply independent model errors.

## ASAL: what was measured?

Source facts: Section 4.2, Figure 5 and Appendix B.2 report exhaustive evaluation of 262,144 Life-like CA rules; each uses 256 random initial states, 2,048 steps, and 32 sampled frames for historical novelty. Conway's rule is in the top 5% under that metric. Section 4.5/Figure 8 compares CLIP, DINOv2 and pixels qualitatively. Appendix B.1 evaluates each single-target candidate on one initial-state seed; Figure 4 illustrates a changed initial state. Appendix B.3 uses 8,192 archive members, 32 offspring per iteration and 100,000 iterations. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799v1#S4.SS2 ; #S4.SS5 ; #A2.SS1 ; #A2.SS2 ; #A2.SS3 .

Supported conclusion: the implementation can locate trajectories novel under a finite-horizon learned visual representation. Untested extension: perpetual innovation, or a generally human-validated definition of complexity. My evaluation: frames are correlated observations, initial states nest within rules, and archive members share ancestry. None substitutes for independent optimizer runs. Using CLIP both to select and visualize success is legitimate optimization but is not independent validation. DINOv2 is an ablation, not automatically a held-out evaluator when it too drives its own search. Single-seed target fitness is vulnerable to seed-specific success.

Proposed test: freeze selected rule/seed parameters and renderer before revealing evaluation seeds. Run 20 independent searches for each of CLIP, pixels, and random selection at matched simulator-step budgets; separately report encoder compute and wall time. Evaluate held-out initial states, 10x longer horizons, and frozen alternative descriptors never used for selection. Blind human raters to method and include periodic and noise controls. Primary endpoint: rate of distinct, persistent behaviors per run after deduplication. Use run-level effects and uncertainty. A collapse after horizon extension falsifies the extrapolation, not the reported short-horizon observation.

## Leniabreeder: finite discovery versus unboundedness

Source facts: Experiments reports ten random-seed replications, population 32,768, Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney tests and Holm–Bonferroni correction. Table 2 median MAP-Elites coverage ranges 38.5–52.2% depending on fitness/descriptor. Figure 5 reports increasing cumulative elite additions after one million evaluations and higher pixel variance for unsupervised fitness, p<0.005. Methods/Search Space initializes from Aquarium; Methods/Unsupervised Statistical Measures trains the eight-dimensional VAE on generated evaluation data. Conclusion acknowledges rotation/scale non-invariance. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04235v1#S5 ; sections Experiments/Manual Diversity, Experiments/Unsupervised Diversity, Methods/Search Space, Methods/Unsupervised Statistical Measures and Conclusion.

Supported conclusion: replicated finite-budget discovery in a specified family of seeded Lenia worlds. Untested extension: unbounded diversity or general robustness across starting families. My evaluation: ten runs are the relevant replication unit. Archive population size is not sample size. A cumulative admission counter cannot decrease even if new behavior stops appearing; a slope must be checked against a stationary replacement control. The adaptive VAE changes the ruler while the population changes. Pixel variance provides another measurement but can reward color/rotation without functional novelty. Coverage percentages depend on grid bounds and should not compare different descriptor spaces directly. No matched random-search superiority estimate is established by the cited table alone.

Proposed test: preregister a hold-out behavioral descriptor independent of each evolving VAE. Compare AURORA, frozen-VAE AURORA, MAP-Elites and random mutation with matched evaluations, identical starting resources, and at least 20 runs per arm; reserve several non-Aquarium initial families for evaluation. Re-score all archived checkpoints with the frozen descriptor after color, rotation and translation normalization, plus damage-recovery/locomotion tests. Report novel viable behaviors per final budget window, extinction/spread failures and effect intervals. Longer runs can support a finite-horizon trend but cannot prove unboundedness.

## From Text to Life: conceptual scope

Source facts: the paper presents a review and analogy, not a new controlled experiment establishing ALife status. ALife for LLMs/Emergence distinguishes hard-coded agent capabilities from bottom-up emergence. Collective Intelligence describes existing multiagent work as mostly short-horizon; Discussion explicitly avoids a definite answer about LLMs as ALife. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09502v1 , sections ALife for LLMs/Emergence, Collective Intelligence and Discussion.

Supported conclusion: useful distinctions and research questions. Untested extension: that adding roles, memory or debate necessarily yields qualitatively new collective intelligence. Proposed test of our platform: equal-total-token single-agent, homogeneous-team and complementary-team arms, randomized across hidden tasks with independent expert scoring. Remove communication or shuffle messages in ablations; freeze held-out tasks before role design. Score unsupported-claim rate, valid falsifications and task accuracy, with compute/latency. Team superiority alone establishes practical value; evidence for interaction requires improvement over budget-matched independent agents plus aggregation, and reproducibility across model families. This protocol is my proposal, not a result of the paper or a completed benchmark.
